The proposal at a glance

Two questions. Up to $615 million.

Question 1

$407.1 million

School rebuilds and additions, a trades FutureReady Center, preservation at North and East, and HVAC and traffic improvements.

More details
  • Build a new trades FutureReady Center on the East High campus for carpentry, HVAC, electrical and plumbing pathways.
  • Rebuild Truesdell Middle School on its existing property.
  • Rebuild Black Elementary to serve students from Black and OK Elementary.
  • Rebuild McLean Elementary with updated learning spaces.
  • Rebuild Chisholm Trail as a K–8 school in Park City.
  • Add a middle-school wing at Cessna Elementary to make it a K–8 school.
  • Preservation work at North High School.
  • Preservation work at East High School.
  • Improve vehicle and pedestrian traffic flow at Christa McAuliffe, Isely and Seltzer.
  • Add HVAC and air-quality improvements at five high schools, eleven middle schools and the Manufacturing FutureReady Center, including air conditioning in gyms and other learning spaces.
  • The ballot also permits other necessary building, technology, efficiency and utility improvements from remaining bond proceeds, plus related fees and expenses.

Question 2 · Additional

$207.9 million

Coleman and Adams rebuilds, building repurposing, early-childhood space, and high-school preservation and career/technical improvements.

More details
  • Preservation work at West, South, Heights and Northwest high schools, including structural repairs and related building maintenance.
  • Renovate the former Hadley Middle School for Horace Mann Dual Language K–8 Magnet, allowing the program to expand.
  • Move Irving Elementary into the current Horace Mann building.
  • Renovate OK Elementary into an early-childhood center to expand Little Early Childhood Center services.
  • South High: add fire-safety labs and expand collision-repair spaces.
  • Northwest High: add law-enforcement pathway spaces.
  • West High: update culinary labs, add a multipurpose space for programs such as JROTC and girls wrestling, and resurface and expand parking.
  • Heights High: expand and renovate woodworking and welding spaces, including paint booths and HVAC, and update the secondary auditorium.
  • At South, Northwest, West and Heights: safety improvements to communications, lighting and storm-shelter vestibule doors, plus auditorium lighting and HVAC optimization as described by the district.
  • Build a new Coleman Middle School, with space for its environmental magnet theme. Renovate the existing building to consolidate Chester Lewis Alternative High School, Education Imagine Academy and Gateway Alternative Program.
  • Rebuild Adams Elementary to continue serving its neighborhood.
  • The ballot also permits other necessary building, technology, efficiency and utility improvements from remaining bond proceeds, plus related fees and expenses.

Assumptions & sources

How this estimate is calculated

Estimates scale from the district’s rounded public examples using your county-appraised residential value:

  • Both questions vs. today: value × 0.00025 per year.
  • Question 1 only vs. today: no estimated change.
  • No bonds vs. today, beginning in 2028: value × −0.000625 per year.

All results compare with today’s rate. The district estimates that if the bond fails, a $200,000 home would pay about $125 less per year beginning with the 2028 tax bill. Maintaining today’s rate is therefore different from receiving that projected decrease. Figures are calculated using annual amounts, then divided by 12 and rounded for display. The district displays $4.16/month for $50/year; standard rounding gives $4.17.

Kansas residential assessed value is 11.5% of appraised value. One mill is $1 per $1,000 of assessed value. This calculator uses the district’s rounded net-change examples, not the gross new-bond levy.

What could change the actual amount?

These estimates hold home value and unrelated tax levies constant. Actual bills depend on appraisals, annual levies, financing terms and the timing of bond issuance. The projected no-bond reduction starts in 2028, not immediately.

The figures are not a year-by-year debt-service forecast. A precise lifetime household cost cannot be calculated by simply multiplying these estimates by 20. Building needs and future funding decisions remain regardless of this election’s outcome.

This calculator does not estimate commercial-property taxes, tax-relief eligibility or rental-cost effects.

Borrowing, interest & repayment

The district-linked election-resolution packet projects $615 million in principal plus approximately $401.7 million in interest for both questions, or approximately $1.017 billion district-wide. Its assumptions include 5% interest, 20-year repayment and no bond state aid.

The packet projects average new-bond levies of 5.500 mills for Question 1 and 2.180 mills for Question 2. Gross new-bond taxes are different from the net change compared with existing taxes.

These are projections, not final financing terms. The publicly linked packet contains unsigned forms and blank template fields.
